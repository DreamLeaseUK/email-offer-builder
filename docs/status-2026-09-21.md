## 3. The rules as of the morning (finder-1.1; §8 takes them to finder-1.3)
- **Tier 1, UK — unchanged.** Same evidence, same order: UK PDF, then the UK web brochure.
- **Tier 2, European fallback — only when tier 1 attached nothing.** The document must be on the manufacturer's
  official host (or linked from its official pages), name the make and model, be a **brochure**, be inside the
  12-month age limit, **be in English** (function-word check against ten European languages; a bilingual file or a
  bare table of figures does not pass), and **say it is European** (its address — `_eu_`, `/eu/`, `/ie/`, `.ie`, a
  European locale — or a € price, or the words Europe / European / EU, or a link from the official pages).
- **Never used:** a European **price & spec guide** (not the UK's prices or trims — Audi Ireland and VW Ireland
  publish exactly these); the **rest of the world** (US, Canada, Australia, NZ, Asia, South Africa…), dropped on the
  address without being opened; a document that **does not say which market it is for**.
- **Dates:** the URL date reader now understands CMS file names — an upload timestamp in front, and a compact
  edition date (YYMMDD, YYYYMMDD, YYYYMM) in the name. The newest of text and URL still wins.
- **Ireland counts as official:** `volkswagen.ie`, `kia.ie` etc. pass the official-host check (the likeliest source
  of an English-language European brochure).
- **Discovery:** European documents the first searches turn up are kept for tier 2 instead of being thrown away
  as "another market". If none is worth opening, tier 2 runs **one** extra search (`<make> <model> brochure Ireland
  Europe`, PDFs only) and opens at most two documents. A failure in tier 2 never undoes the tier 1 result.
- **Cost:** a document read for tier 1 that failed on UK evidence alone is re-judged for tier 2 from the same
  reading (never paid for twice). Same 25-credit cap.
- **What comes back:** still one of the six outcomes. A fallback is `verified_pdf` / `verified_web_brochure` with
  **`market: 'eu'`**, the flag `european_edition` (plus `euro_pricing` when it shows € prices). The stored record
  carries `market: 'eu'`, is titled **"… brochure (European edition)"** (never "(UK)") and its note says no UK edition
  was verified. `Brochure.market` / `BrochureSearch.market` are new optional schema fields (JSON snapshots: no D1
  migration).
- **Old "nothing found" results are not remembered across a rules change:** a remembered negative made by an older
  finder version is searched again (this is why Polestar 2 re-ran without pressing "Search again").
- **The rep sees it:** the attached panel shows an orange "European edition" note (what it is, that specification,
  equipment and any prices are not the UK's, "replace it if you have the UK one"); the trace says why it was accepted.
- **The recipient is told (the disclaimer, Matt 21 Sept):** when the attached brochure has `market: 'eu'` the card's
  small print gains, after "Brochure figures are the manufacturer's and may differ from this offer.": **"This is the
  manufacturer's European brochure; specification, equipment and prices may differ from UK models."** grid3's one
  shared footnote gains "Where a brochure is the manufacturer's European edition, specification, equipment and prices
  may differ from UK models." Email, plain text and hosted page alike. It is data-side text in `render`'s viewmodel
  (`EUROPEAN_BROCHURE_NOTE`), like the sentence it follows: v5 markup and `MARKUP_VERSION` untouched. The link label
  is unchanged ("Download brochure (PDF)"). **Wording is Matt's; Emma has not approved it yet** (brief §5.8 step 9:
  she approves brochure small print with the template block).

## 9. Evening: finder-1.4 — search discovers, Map and Scrape understand the site, the page is operated, THEN validation
Matt rejected the incremental plan and supplied an analysis: the finder was judging documents from search metadata
before anything had looked at the page a person would use. Its points were taken as the design.

**What changed (`finder.ts`, new `operate.ts`, `client.ts`, `ensure.ts`, schema):**
- **Map, not guessed paths.** Once the official UK site is known, Firecrawl Map (1 credit) returns this model's page
  and the site's brochure / download / price pages. The five fixed paths and the `site:` search remain only as the
  fallback when Map returns nothing.
- **The model's own page is opened FIRST**, and chosen properly: every spelling the variants allow ("E-208" / "208"),
  never the fleet, business, Motability, offers, news or tutorial page, never another model's page, and the page
  whose last path segment is the model and little else (not a trim's page).
- **The page is OPERATED, not read** (`operate.ts`): ONE Firecrawl scrape with `actions`, **1 credit**, runs in
  Firecrawl's own browser — hooks `window.open`, `a.click()`, `fetch`, XHR and link clicks, dismisses a cookie
  banner, notes the rendered links, presses the controls labelled brochure / download / specification / price list
  one at a time, and reports each file with HOW it was reached and the LABEL that led to it. It never presses
  request / test-drive / configurator controls and never fills in or submits anything. (Playwright cannot run inside
  the Cloudflare Worker; Firecrawl's actions do the same job through the API already in use.)
- **The page is evidence.** A document reached from the model's page need not name the model in its file name, and
  the model's page vouches for the model when the brochure styles the name its own way. "Reached from a page of the
  official UK site" is a UK signal in its own right.
- **A score no longer stops a plausible official document being opened.** Provenance does the gating (official host,
  or linked from the official site); the score only orders the queue. Brochures are opened before price guides, and
  a file that names exactly this model before a generic "download" redirect.
- **Bare "Download" links** are read by the heading above them (which car) and the words before them (what it is):
  Škoda's brochure page is a list of headings each followed by "Brochures & pricelist pdf (9 MB) [Download]".
- **Another model's document is refused by name**: "c-hr-plus.pdf" is the C-HR+, not the C-HR; also after a download
  link has been resolved. A '+' glued to the model is a name, a '+' between words is a space ("KONA+Brochure").
- **Not sales literature**: Motability guides, offer terms, T&Cs, accessibility / gender-pay reports, HomeCharge and
  roadside documents are dropped on their name.
- **A "web brochure" that is a form is a brochure REQUEST** (BMW's RFI pages, Toyota's "order a brochure").
- **Age by provenance.** What the manufacturer's own UK site is serving today IS its current edition: up to 36 months
  for a brochure and 12 for a price guide when it is on the official UK host / path (or the official European one
  in the fallback) or linked from the official site, flagged `older_edition` past the ordinary 12 / 6 and shown to
  the rep; the ordinary limits still apply to anything else. When the first acceptable document is an older
  edition the search carries on for a newer one and takes the newest. An undated official file is dated by its
  `Last-Modified` header.
- **The official host rule is wider**: the make with a generic word after it (automobiles, motorcars, motor, global,
  gb, europe…), a known word before it (saic…), or the model after it (ineosgrenadier); and a result that describes
  itself as UK counts where the address carries no UK marker. Dealers and press offices still fail.
- **A discovery miss is not an answer.** `BrochureSearch.exhausted` is false when no page of the official site was
  ever opened; such a result is held for 1 day, not 7. The trace now records, per document, every way it was
  discovered (`discoveredBy`), the action taken (`pressed "Download Geely EX2 Brochure"`) and why the site was
  taken to be official (`officialWhy`).

  that the same evening: §10.)

## 11. 22 Sept: why the price arrives black — Outlook's paste mode, not the markup
Evidence: the sent copy of Matt's "test mobile 6" (downloaded from Outlook's Sent Items as `.eml`) and the same
message as Gmail received it. Every text run left Outlook as a `<div class="skipProofing">` carrying the account's
default font: `font-family:Arial; font-size:12pt; color:rgb(0,0,0)`. Our `<p>` / `<span>` colour and size were gone
and bold had become `<b>`; line-height, margins, letter-spacing, `td` backgrounds and link colours were untouched
(`<a class="lock-red" style="color:rgb(227,6,19)">`, the button's white). That is Outlook's **Merge formatting**
paste, and Outlook on the web showed the setting: Settings → Mail → Compose and reply → Cut, copy and paste →
"Pasting from emails and calendar: Keep source formatting", **"Pasting from other apps: Merge formatting"**. The
tool is an "other app".

Proven through Outlook's own editor (Outlook on the web, the same editor as New Outlook, fed our markup and read
back):
- Under Merge formatting **no construction keeps a font size**: `<span style>`, `<p style>`, `<font size>`, `<h1>`,
  `<big>`, `<b style>`, a Word-flavoured document, an Excel-flavoured table and Outlook's own `elementToProof`
  markup all come back 12pt black. Only a link keeps its colour (`<a href style="color">`), never its size.
- Under **Keep source formatting** the same price markup comes back `<span style="color:rgb(227,6,19);
  font-size:21pt">` (21pt = 28px): red and big, as designed. The `<style>` block and the conditional comments are
  dropped in every mode, so the 21 Sept design rule (§7.3) stands.
- Nothing in the HTML makes Outlook treat a paste as "from emails and calendar": `elementToProof`, OWA ids,
  `StartFragment` markers and an Outlook `Generator` meta changed nothing.

**Fix:** the rep pastes with Keep source formatting. Per paste: the "(Ctrl)" paste-options button under the pasted
email → Keep source formatting. Once: Settings → Mail → Compose and reply → Cut, copy and paste → Pasting from other
apps → Keep source formatting (the paste-options menu's "Set default paste" does the same). **Matt to confirm with a
real send.** The Copy for Outlook screen now says so in its helper line under the button (`Compose.tsx`, 22 Sept). The
markup is unchanged.
